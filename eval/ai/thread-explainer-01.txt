Mercury retrograde is one of the most talked-about astrological events, but what does it actually mean? Let's break it down.

From an astronomical perspective, Mercury retrograde is an optical illusion. Three or four times a year, Mercury appears to move backward across the sky as seen from Earth. This happens because Mercury orbits the Sun much faster than our planet does, so at certain points in its orbit, it seems to reverse direction relative to the background stars.

In astrology, however, this period carries significant symbolic weight. Mercury is traditionally associated with communication, technology, travel, and thought. When it goes retrograde, many believe these areas of life can become more prone to misunderstandings, delays, and technical glitches. People often advise double-checking emails, backing up important files, and avoiding signing major contracts during this time.

It's worth noting that retrograde periods aren't inherently negative. Many astrologers view them as opportunities for reflection, revision, and reconnecting with the past. It can be an ideal time to revisit unfinished projects or reach out to old friends.

Whether you take it seriously or simply find it fun, Mercury retrograde offers a fascinating blend of science and symbolism.
