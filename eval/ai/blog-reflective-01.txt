There is something quietly transformative about the first cold morning of autumn. The air shifts, carrying with it the scent of fallen leaves and distant woodsmoke, and suddenly the world seems to slow down just enough for us to notice it again.

I have always found this season to be a time of reflection. As the trees let go of their leaves, I find myself thinking about what I, too, might release. Old habits that no longer serve me. Grudges I have carried far longer than necessary. The relentless pressure to always be doing more.

This year, I made a small promise to myself: to walk every morning before checking my phone. At first, it felt almost impossible. The pull of notifications was stronger than I wanted to admit. But slowly, something changed. The walks became a ritual, a space to breathe and simply be present.

I noticed things I had overlooked for years. The way sunlight filters through the maple on the corner. The neighbor who waves every single day without fail. The gentle rhythm of my own footsteps on the pavement.

Autumn reminds us that letting go is not a loss but a necessary part of growth. Every ending carries within it the seed of a new beginning. As the days grow shorter, I am learning to embrace the quiet, to find beauty in stillness, and to trust that spring will return in its own time.
