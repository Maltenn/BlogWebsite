My writing tends to start with questions. I find it hard to dive straight into a topic that interests me, and a question hands me a problem to solve and something to understand. It is the same reason I love a thought experiment. It gives me license to unravel a subject until I feel content, a gift of a question already shaped with such nuance that I get to argue with myself over something that often has no answer. That may be the justification I am always seeking: a general agreement that the topic deserves analysis. If I wasn’t always chasing approval from other’s to explore my thoughts, I might not need questions like these. It has already been agreed upon as worthy by everyone who debated the idea before me, which frees me from the judgment of others and leaves me alone to tinker. I frequently find myself thinking about the Ship of Theseus.
