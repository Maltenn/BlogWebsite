Comments about how if “those” people just worked as hard as they had, they would be as successful. Crime statistics rattled off for them to laugh at from a throne in a sanctuary built by their lineage. As I grew to resent the church, the Republican talking points, and the justifications for bigotry, my guilt over my association with my own family grew right alongside. Despite all of this, I still went to my grandparents’ house and sat talking with them, eating salsa made from tomatoes and peppers they had grown. They complained that this year’s harvest of peppers was abnormally spicy. They complained about it every year. I think their tolerance for spice was just fading as they aged, but I never cared to tell them. I didn’t want to admit it, because I loved them, and the truth might hurt them. I carried that unresolved tension for years, and luckily for me, I moved to Brooklyn before I reached any conclusions, unbuckling the restraints the Bible Belt had kept me in for far too long.
