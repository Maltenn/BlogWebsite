They told anecdotes about how I used to act, things I used to do, examples of who I was. Sitting in front of them, I could tell they didn’t realize I wasn’t that person anymore. Or maybe I was, and I had simply lost the ability to connect the dots between the grandson they knew and the one sitting across from them. I have always considered myself reserved to a fault. No one will ever come close to knowing me completely and truly. But maybe I am naïve to think that too, because I was astonished at how effortlessly they held the past up to me like a mirror, without even trying. When the evening ended and it was time for the hour-and-a-half ride home on the Q train, I said my goodbyes and waved as I turned to walk away. Then I texted my girlfriend: “we had a great visit, and the food was so good!”
