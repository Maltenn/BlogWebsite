Once more than half of it was new? There is no correct answer. Still, the hypothetical always resonated with me in its literal, physical sense. It wasn’t until recently, at 24, that I started trying to educate myself in philosophy, and more recently still that the paradox surfaced again in my head, this time about myself. When I had considered it in that light before, I only had the body in mind, which makes 300 to 400 billion new cells every day. Clearly that doesn’t make me someone entirely new, right? But what if the parts stop being physical? What if time and lived experience begin replacing the pieces of who I was, plank by plank, and the sum of those replacements is who I am? I have spent my life dissatisfied with who I am. My family set their expectations intentionally high, so high they were unachievable, justified by the notion that if there was always room for improvement, their son would have nowhere to go but up, and they would end up with a son they could be proud of.
