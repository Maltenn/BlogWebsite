I began my journey home satisfied, with a kind of connection I hadn’t felt in a long time. That contentment didn’t last. Sitting on the train, living inside my own head, I reflected on the evening. Across from me sat a family: a father with his son and daughter. They were joking, bumping into each other to tease, filled with laughter, and breaking from their playful patterns to have small, serious conversations about what was happening in their lives. Before I even knew I was sad, tears began to fill my eyes. Fitting, and a little ironic, that someone who rejects where they came from would earn their official New Yorker card by crying on the subway on a night like this. I already missed them. It had been less than an hour since I’d left my grandparents, and I couldn’t help feeling that I needed more. The family across from me had no clue that their ordinary evening was carrying me back to times in my life I hadn’t thought about since they stopped occurring.
