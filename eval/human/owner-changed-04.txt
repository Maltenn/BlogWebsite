It was a trade I was unaware I was making, a contract signed in my name before I could read it, and it is far too late to void. Where that leaves me now is pessimistic and eternally dissatisfied, chasing what I foolishly believe is achievable: to be enough. Every year I look back at who I was the year before, and every year I see a fool. I have always credited this to my desire to improve and chase higher heights, even as I felt nothing but shortfall. I considered it a good trait, and in many ways it is. What I was blind to, with my eyes fixed on the lights above me, was everything I was leaving behind. How can you be someone if you never stay someone? All my life I have seen only who I could be, never who I am. When I left my hometown in rural Georgia, what excited me most wasn’t Brooklyn itself, the big city with its endless opportunity.
