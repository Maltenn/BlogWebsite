In my new life, distance from the old one was something I engineered. The resentment I once carried slid from the front of my mind, because the characters most woven into it were no longer around. I never reached a conclusion. I never finished reconciling. Finally under a roof I provided for myself, I didn’t have to appease anyone else’s expectations of me. What came of that, though unintentional, was clearly inevitable: I forgot who I was in Georgia. What had been the overwhelming majority of my life doesn’t feel, to this day, like it ever happened. I recall my memories like facts from a textbook, with nothing behind my eyes to illustrate them. It feels like I have always been in Brooklyn. Not in the metaphorical, “this is where I am supposed to be” sense, but in a much more abstract sense, a mental distancing from who I was, where I was, and who surrounded me.
