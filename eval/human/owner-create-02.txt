There I was, twelve, awkward and insufferable, and, like nearly every time I have changed since, it started with a pretty girl who saw past the naïve boy in front of her. She saw not who I was but who I could become. I was too young to know better and curious enough to change anyway. Whether she meant to or not, Lucy sits at the root of most of what I am today. Follow almost any branch of me down far enough and you find her. Lucy never told me whether I should stay religious. She only told me to think about why I was. It was idiotic to base your life around a concept you had never stopped to question. So simple, but to me at the time, was as foreign as a concept could get. I started reading, obsessively, everything I could find online about why people stayed in their religion or left it.
