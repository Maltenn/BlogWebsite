I rarely cry, normally once or twice a year, which is an improvement on my previous pattern of once every year or two. When I do, it is usually because I am overwhelmed by more emotions than I can logically rationalize and sort into their proper boxes in my head. This was that, fused with a revelation about myself. It wasn’t until a few days of sitting with those emotions that I began to unpack the lessons they were teaching me. The first lesson was the careful dissection I am responsible for: separating my family’s beliefs from the kind of people they are. My family, just like me, has an abundance of faults. I had let myself fall into the mindset of cancel culture, into the overwhelming sense that it is my job, as someone privileged, to speak up for those less fortunate than me. Because the evangelical think tank had harmed me, I decided it was acceptable to resent everyone involved.
